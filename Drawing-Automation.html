Drawing Automation

I helped engineers save time by automating the creation of technical drawings and paperwork.

The challenge

Engineers were spending almost half their time on manual drafting tasks. It was a huge bottleneck and left a lot of room for mistakes.

My role

UX Lead. When I joined, the team had already been working for six months. I helped them refocus on user needs to deliver capabilities within the next six months.

The result

Drawing Automation saved engineers about 40% of their time so they could focus on more important work.

The scope: automating the mundane

Target the most repetitive, time-consuming parts of creating 2D documentation:

  • Dimensioning and annotation placement
  • View creation, rotation, and placement
  • BOM generation and placement

The business case: scaling through efficiency

The investment in Drawing Automation wasn't just about saving time; it was a strategic move to enable our customers to scale. Automating 2D documentation directly impacts ROI by increasing shop-floor capacity and reducing error-related production costs.

Design solutions

Wireframes

Phase 4

Evaluation

Feedback loop: validation through iteration

I tested the ideas rather than relying on guesses. By bringing in Lighthouse Customers early, I put our wireframes and prototypes to the test. This helped maintain a high “trust score” throughout the process, across multiple design sprints.

  • Weekly design critiques with the Dev Architect and Product Manager
  • Bi-weekly usability sessions with lead engineers
  • Real-time feedback through dedicated Slack channels

The heuristic audit

I started with a deep-dive audit to understand what the team had done before I joined, and to identify issues that needed to be addressed before the first public release.

Issue 1

Cognitive load

The original settings interface was cluttered with too many inputs. For engineers working quickly, this made things difficult. I redesigned it to show only the information users actually need to control.

Issue 2

Transparency

Jon was most worried about not knowing what the machine was doing. To address this, we added a live sheet count. Now engineers can see exactly what will happen before they click “Generate.”

Issue 3

Accuracy

Automation only helps if it works correctly. I found that the system was hitting a set limit and missing important dimensions. I teamed up with others to fix these settings and, just as important, made sure users could adjust the limits, so Jon would never feel the system was letting him down.

The next sprint

While initial excitement was high, the engagement plan revealed specific technical hurdles:

Performance benchmarks. Users reported lag on massive assemblies, leading us to optimize engine speed.

Documentation gaps. We found edge cases where complex sheets were skipped during batch runs.

Recipe customization. Requests to pre-select dimensioning styles based on sheet classification.

Model readiness. Requests for pre-flight checks to make sure 3D models are ready for automation.

Leadership

Hurdles cleared along the way

Technical debt

Building on a legacy system meant every decision had to be weighed against performance. I worked with the architects to make the automation feel fast, even with complex data sets.

Expectation management

Leadership wanted “push-button” magic; users wanted a tool they could trust. I navigated this disagree-and-commit situation by showing that trust is earned through transparency.

Team alignment

Joining 26 weeks late was challenging. I built trust with the team through the heuristic audit, so they could see I was there to help them finish, not to hold them back.

Complex compliance

Technical drawings have zero margin for error. I made sure our automation logic honored the strict legal and standards requirements of manufacturing.

Retrospective

Final thoughts

This project was a masterclass in leading from within. Joining 26 weeks into a development cycle meant there was no time for a slow ramp-up. I had to prove the value of UX through immediate, actionable insights like the heuristic audit and the engagement plan.

It reinforced a core belief of mine: even in the most technical, automation-heavy environments, trust is the primary currency. If the user doesn't trust the logic, the speed of the engine doesn't matter.

Bridging the trust gap

The biggest hurdle was the gap between business desires and customer readiness. Leadership wanted a “black box” that did the work for them; users like Jon wanted a tool that helped them work faster.

By advocating for transparency (exposing sheet counts, allowing manual overrides, and showing the intended logic before execution) we didn't just build a tool. We built a system engineers were actually irrationally excited to use.

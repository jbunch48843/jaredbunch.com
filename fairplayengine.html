Fair Play Lineup

I applied my enterprise design experience to a common youth-sports problem: a tool that helps coaches build lineups that are fair and follow league rules.

R&D note: AI-assisted iterationThis is a rapid, first-iteration concept developed in partnership with AI (Figma Make), which I used to turn complex logic into a high-fidelity interactive prototype quickly.

The challenge

Volunteer coaches struggle to balance coaching and teaching with managing a live game, while staying fair and following league rules.

My role

Sole designer. I defined the logic layer and IA, and used AI to accelerate high-fidelity prototyping.

AI partnership

With Figma Make as a design partner, I quickly built UI scaffolding that fit my logic constraints, and used AI to brainstorm and explore the problem space.

The result

A constraint-based tool that automates fair rotations, enforces league rules, and provides transparent post-game reporting.

Phase 1

Discovery & target

The target user

Meet Kristin and Keith. They represent the volunteer coaches who manage lineups and try to stay within league rules. They have their hands full balancing full-time jobs with coaching duties. They're busy teaching kids the game, and on game days they can easily miss that they aren't following their league's playing-time rules, or simply lose track of things in the chaos.

Validation roadmap

This is a quick, first iteration of an idea to solve a real-world problem I've experienced personally. I haven't done research yet; this is my high-level plan for when I do.

5+ inquiries

Interview coaches and run moderated feedback sessions where coaches get hands-on with the proposed solution.

Surveys

Quantify rule variations across different regions.

League rules audit

Identify initial markets and leagues, and verify the constraint logic meets all targeted requirements.

Phase 2

Establishing the logic

Constraint-based logic

I treated league rules as part of the logic behind the game, not just a list of settings. Setting up system constraints early helped me avoid problems later and stopped illegal rotations before they could happen.

The logic prompt

I started with a blank canvas and focused on giving the AI system rules instead of worrying about how it looked. I gave it an initial prompt, then kept adding, changing, or removing prompts until I got the result you see here.

  • Rules-based logic and limits for the lineup
  • High-contrast outdoor legibility
  • Customization
// Input to Figma Make

"Design a mobile interface where coaches can define their roster and then build a lineup for softball based off of their roster. Drag n drop placement as well as click interactions should be supported."

"We will have a customizable rules section that will be editable by coaches and used/checked when the lineups are built. Some rules to start with are: number of inning limit for position and bench, player must play minimum 2 inngins in the field, optional rule that only qualified players can be placed at pitcher and catcher positions, no player should sit on the bench for 2 consecutive innings."

"This will be used in bright sunlight, so we will need a high-contrast option the user can toggle on/off to increase visibility in these scenarios."

Phase 3

Managing the lineup

Youth sports rosters change quickly. I built the roster system so coaches can easily handle last-minute absences or changes, and have information about their players at their fingertips.

The lineup

At a glance, the coach can see each player's qualifications, who has a strong arm, and which positions they've been assigned to in each inning.

Phase 4

Reducing cognitive load

Quick changes happen during a live game. I designed a drag-and-drop system with real-time feedback so coaches can make changes quickly while still following league rules. During iteration I also added a click-click workflow to limit scrolling. I'm still not happy with where this is, and will keep iterating on the behavior and layout.

Designing for the unknown

I built a system that can handle unexpected changes during a game. The goal was to help coaches adapt quickly to last-minute changes without breaking the rules or creating unfair situations for players.

Phase 5

Closing the loop

Post-game reports verify compliance and highlight player development. That transparency builds trust with parents and makes sure the league's fair-play mission is actually being met.

Building accountability

A data-backed record of every inning played turns a potentially emotional conversation with a parent into a factual one. It moves the coach from “guessing” to “knowing,” which is the primary goal of the Fair Play Engine.

Retrospective

Reflections & roadmap

The first iteration of Fair Play Lineup handles the core logic of rotations, and I've already identified several opportunities to deepen the experience. My focus for the next version is to move from a standalone tool to a connected ecosystem.

The AI advantage

Using AI for the initial UI scaffolding let me spend my time on the high-level logic and information architecture. It shifted the focus from “how does this button look” to “how does this engine think.”

  • 40% faster prototyping
  • Immediate translation of logic to UI
  • Rapid exploration of high-contrast themes

Technical hurdles

The biggest challenge was the “game-time pivot.” Staying compliant while a coach is frantically removing players required a robust, constraint-based architecture that feels invisible to the user.

What's next?

Account persistence

Let league officials push pre-verified rosters and rules directly to a coach's account to reduce setup time.

AI integration

AI features that help coaches build lineups from defensive and offensive performance metrics.

Offline reliability

Make sure the local database syncs flawlessly even in parks with zero cell reception, a common pain point for Keith.

  • Season support: track player development, playing time, positions played, and attendance across a whole season.
  • Multi-coach sync: real-time updates so assistants and head coaches stay aligned.
  • Centralized rules: leagues set global fairness rules that teams adopt automatically.
  • Coach's notes: private observations on player development that can be toggled on or off for parent reports.
  • League integration: automate the paperwork by syncing with league roster databases.
  • Performance data: integrate with scoring apps so older age groups can balance performance with fair play.
